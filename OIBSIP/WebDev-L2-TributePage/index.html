Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

The Missile Man of India and 11th President of India

Dr. Kalam during an official engagement, reflecting his vision for youth and science.

Biography

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Coming from a humble background, his early life was characterized by hard work and determination as he sold newspapers to support his family's income while pursuing his education.

He studied physics and aerospace engineering, eventually spending over four decades as a scientist and science administrator, mainly at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). He was intimately involved in India's civilian space programme and military missile development efforts.

In 2002, Dr. Kalam was elected as the 11th President of India with the support of both the ruling party and the opposition. Widely referred to as the "People's President," he spent his post-presidency life visiting educational institutions and encouraging millions of students across the nation.

Key Achievements

  • 1969: Transferred to ISRO as project director of India's first Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III).
  • 1998: Played a pivotal organizational, technical, and political role in the Pokhran-II nuclear tests.
  • 1997: Awarded India's highest civilian honor, the Bharat Ratna.
  • 2002–2007: Served as the 11th President of India.
